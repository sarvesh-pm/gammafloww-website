**A put option gives its holder the right — but not the obligation — to sell an asset at a fixed strike price on or before an expiry date, in exchange for a premium.** You buy a put when you expect the price to fall, or to protect a position you already hold. If the price drops, your right to sell high is valuable; if it doesn't, you lose only the premium. Here's how puts work and why crypto traders use them.

## The mechanics

Like a call, a put has a **strike price**, an **expiry**, and a **premium** — but it's the right to *sell*, not buy. If you own a Bitcoin put with a $70,000 strike and BTC falls to $60,000, your right to sell at $70,000 is worth $10,000 of intrinsic value — the put is "in the money" ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/p/putoption.asp)). If BTC stays above the strike, the put expires worthless and your loss is limited to the premium.

## Two reasons to buy a put

- **To bet on a fall** — a put profits as the underlying drops below the strike, with risk capped at the premium (unlike a short position, whose losses can run).
- **To hedge** — this is the big one. If you hold spot Bitcoin, buying a put acts like insurance: it sets a floor under your position, so a crash is cushioned by the gain on the put. It's a core [hedging](/blog/hedging-crypto) tool.

## Puts in crypto

Puts trade on crypto [options platforms](/blog/crypto-options-trading-platform) alongside calls, most liquidly on Bitcoin and Ether. Their value responds to the same [Greeks](/blog/options-greeks-explained) as any option — and because crypto is so volatile, the "insurance premium" on a crypto put can be expensive, especially heading into known catalysts.

<Callout title="A put is portfolio insurance">
Think of a protective put like insuring a car: you pay a premium, and if nothing bad happens you're out that cost — but if a crash comes, the payout cushions the loss. Long-term holders use puts to stay in the market through volatility without being forced to sell the underlying.
</Callout>

## Put vs. call

A put profits when price falls; its opposite, the [call option](/blog/what-is-a-call-option), profits when price rises. Buying a put is a defined-risk alternative to shorting with [leverage](/blog/crypto-leverage-explained), where a sharp rally could otherwise liquidate you. See where options fit among crypto products in [Perps vs. Options vs. Dated Futures](/blog/perpetual-futures-vs-options-vs-dated-futures).

## The takeaway

A put option is the right to sell at a set price by a set date, for a premium — a defined-risk way to bet on a fall or to hedge a holding you don't want to sell. It pays off when price drops below the strike, and expires worthless if it doesn't. Master calls and puts and you have the two atoms from which every options strategy is built.
