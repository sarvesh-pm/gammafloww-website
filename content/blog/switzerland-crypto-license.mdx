**There is no single "Switzerland crypto license" — the country regulates crypto activity by its economic function under technology-neutral financial-market law, so your path depends on what you actually do, not the word "crypto."** For most operators that means either self-regulatory-organization (SRO) membership or direct FINMA authorization. Here's how the framework works and which path fits a trading venue.

## The core principle: function over label

Switzerland doesn't have a bespoke crypto regime; it applies its existing financial-market laws to the *economic substance* of an activity. A token that behaves like a security is treated as a security; a service that behaves like deposit-taking is regulated as such ([CMS Law](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-to-crypto-regulation/switzerland)). This is why "do I need a license?" always resolves to "what, precisely, are you doing?"

## The two main paths

**1. SRO membership (AMLA).** For most exchanges, brokers, and OTC desks, the usual route is joining a FINMA-recognized self-regulatory organization to meet anti-money-laundering obligations under the AMLA ([Gofaizen-Sherle](https://gofaizen-sherle.com/crypto-license/switzerland)). This is the more accessible path for many trading operators.

**2. Direct FINMA authorization.** Required for activities involving public deposits, full custody, tokenized securities trading, publicly redeemable stablecoins, or operating a DLT trading venue ([FINMA](https://www.finma.ch/en/authorisation/fintech/)). This is a heavier lift with capital, governance, and organizational requirements.

## The DLT Act and the DLT trading facility

Switzerland's **DLT Act**, fully in force since 1 August 2021, modernized the law for tokenized assets and created a new infrastructure category — the **DLT trading facility** — for multilateral trading of DLT-based securities, bridging exchange and custody functions under one authorization ([Lindemann Law](https://lindemannlaw.ch/expertise/blockchain-crypto-fintech-artificial-intelligence-technology/token-and-dlt-regulation-in-switzerland-2/)). It's what makes Switzerland attractive for regulated tokenized-securities venues specifically.

<Callout title="What this means for a trading venue">
If you're running a crypto brokerage or exchange, you'll most likely start with SRO membership for AML, and step up to FINMA authorization if you take custody, deposits, or trade tokenized securities. Derivatives and securities-like products push you toward the heavier authorizations — scope your product precisely before assuming a path.
</Callout>

## Switzerland in the global picture

Switzerland is one of several credible hubs; where it fits depends on your product and target market. Compare it against other jurisdictions in the [Global Licensing Guide](/blog/crypto-derivatives-licensing-global-guide) and the EU's [MiCA regime](/blog/mica-crypto-derivatives-casp-license) — and remember licensing sits on top of your [KYC/AML](/blog/kyc-aml-for-crypto-exchanges) obligations, not instead of them.

## The takeaway

A "Swiss crypto license" is really a question of which regime your activity triggers: SRO membership for AML in most cases, direct FINMA authorization for custody, deposits, or tokenized securities, and the DLT Act's trading facility for regulated tokenized-asset venues. Scope your economic function first — the license follows from what you do.
