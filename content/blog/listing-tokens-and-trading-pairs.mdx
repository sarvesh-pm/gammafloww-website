Deciding what trades on your venue looks like a growth lever — more pairs, more volume — but it's really a risk decision. A bad listing can expose you to a security-classification problem, a hacked contract, or a thin, manipulable market that damages trust. The "wild west" era of effortless listings is over; the 2026 bar is a genuine due-diligence gauntlet ([The Signal](https://thesignal.directory/intelligence/crypto-exchange-listing-process-costs-2026)). Here's the operator's pipeline.

## The listing pipeline

| Stage | What you're checking |
|---|---|
| **1. Due diligence** | Legal classification, contract audit, team, tokenomics |
| **2. Liquidity readiness** | Market maker in place, depth, spreads |
| **3. Technical integration** | Wallet/custody, deposits/withdrawals, network support |
| **4. Ongoing risk** | Surveillance, delisting triggers, monitoring |

### 1. Due diligence
This is the gate. Expect to require a **legal opinion** on whether the token is a security or a commodity in the markets you serve; a **smart-contract audit** (firms like CertiK or Hacken, with all critical/high issues resolved); and review of the **team, tokenomics, vesting and concentration** ([Token Market Maker](https://www.tokenmarketmaker.io/cex-token-listing-requirements-2026/)). Listing compliance is now a formal standard, not a favour ([Midlands in Business](https://midlandsinbusiness.com/token-listing-compliance-due-diligence-standards-for-crypto-exchanges)).

### 2. Liquidity readiness
A listing with no depth is worse than no listing — users hit slippage and leave. Require a **market-making commitment** and target tight spreads and consistent depth before you go live. An emerging Tier-1 practice is mandating independent, third-party market depth ("proof of liquidity provision") so a token doesn't list into a vacuum.

### 3. Technical integration
The asset has to actually move: wallet and [custody](/blog/crypto-exchange-custody-models) support, deposit/withdrawal flows, correct network/chain support (and the right confirmations), and monitoring for reorgs or bridge risk.

<Callout title="Derivatives listings are a different bar">
Listing a *perpetual* on an asset is harder than listing spot. You need a **reliable price feed** — a robust index across liquid venues and a manipulation-resistant [mark price](/blog/mark-price-index-price-oracles) — or your funding and liquidations can be gamed. Thin-underlying perps are a classic manipulation vector; be conservative about which assets get leverage.
</Callout>

### 4. Ongoing risk
Listing isn't the end. Wire the pair into [market surveillance](/blog/market-surveillance-wash-trading), define **delisting triggers** (collapsed volume, security incident, regulatory change), and keep AML monitoring on deposits of the new asset.

## The takeaway

Run listings as a risk pipeline, not a growth checkbox: clear the legal and audit due-diligence gate, secure real liquidity before launch, integrate custody and network support properly, and — for derivatives — insist on a manipulation-resistant price feed. A disciplined listing process protects the one thing a new exchange can't easily rebuild: trader trust. Pair it with your [liquidity](/blog/crypto-exchange-liquidity-cold-start) and [compliance](/blog/kyc-aml-for-crypto-exchanges) foundations.
