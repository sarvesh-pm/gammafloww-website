**Crypto exchange liquidity solutions are the ways a new venue gets tradeable depth into its order book** — because an exchange with no liquidity is a ghost town, and traders leave the moment they see wide spreads or thin books. The main options are professional market makers, liquidity aggregation, and liquidity-as-a-service bundled with your platform. Here's how each works and when to use it.

## Why liquidity is the hardest launch problem

Liquidity is a chicken-and-egg trap: traders won't come without depth, and market makers won't quote without traders. A brand-new book starts empty, so **someone has to provide the first depth** — this is the [cold-start problem](/blog/crypto-exchange-liquidity-cold-start), and it's the single biggest reason new venues stall. Deep, tight order books attract flow; thin ones repel it ([XBTO](https://www.xbto.com/resources/how-market-makers-provide-liquidity-and-stabilize-crypto-markets)).

## The main solutions

**1. Professional market makers.** Firms that continuously quote both sides of the book, earning the spread and any rebates. They're the primary source of real depth — you either run this [in-house or contract an external firm](/blog/market-making-internal-vs-external), each with different cost and control trade-offs ([Dappfort](https://www.dappfort.com/blog/attract-liquidity-providers-crypto-exchange/)).

**2. Liquidity aggregation.** Instead of building depth from scratch, you pull quotes from external venues or liquidity providers and present a combined book to your traders. It bootstraps depth quickly, but you depend on the aggregated sources and their pricing ([Dappfort](https://www.dappfort.com/blog/crypto-liquidity-solutions-for-exchange-startups/)).

**3. Liquidity-as-a-service (LaaS).** Your platform provider supplies connected market-maker relationships and/or a shared liquidity pool as part of the package, so you launch on existing depth rather than negotiating every relationship yourself ([Openware](https://www.openware.com/solution/crypto-market-making-and-liquidity-services)). For derivatives specifically, this is often the fastest route to a book that looks and trades like an established venue.

<Callout title="Liquidity is a system, not a switch">
Real depth comes from market makers who trust your venue — which means they need a fast, deterministic [matching engine](/blog/inside-a-crypto-matching-engine) and predictable latency to quote tightly. Jittery execution makes them widen spreads to protect themselves, which worsens liquidity for everyone. Infrastructure quality and liquidity are the same problem.
</Callout>

## How to evaluate a liquidity solution

- **Depth and spread** on the pairs you actually plan to run — not a demo book.
- **Who provides it** — named market makers vs. anonymous aggregated feeds.
- **Cost model** — rebates, fees, or revenue-share, and who bears inventory risk.
- **Reliability under volatility** — does depth hold when the market moves, or vanish when you need it most?

If you're buying a platform, liquidity should be part of the [provider due diligence](/blog/how-to-choose-white-label-exchange-provider), not an afterthought — a great engine with an empty book still loses traders.

## The takeaway

The best liquidity solution depends on your stage: most new venues combine a professional market maker with either aggregation or a provider's liquidity-as-a-service to launch on real depth, then deepen relationships as volume grows. Whatever you choose, the book has to be tight from day one — traders judge an exchange in the first few seconds of looking at it.
