A large share of the world's crypto derivatives volume has historically been booked through offshore entities. The appeal is obvious — lighter-touch regimes, lower capital, faster setup, favourable tax. But "offshore" in 2026 is a very different proposition than it was even two years ago: Seychelles, the BVI and the Cayman Islands have all introduced or tightened virtual-asset regimes, and the era of the truly unregulated derivatives venue is over. Here's an honest map of the main routes and their trade-offs.

## The main offshore routes

| Jurisdiction | Regime | What it permits | Indicative capital |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Seychelles** | FSA — Securities Dealer License; VASP Act 2024 | Securities Dealer covers forex/CFDs/derivatives incl. crypto-CFDs; VASP Act covers exchange/custody | ~US$100k (Securities Dealer) |
| **BVI** | FSC — VASP Act 2022 | Registration as a virtual-asset service provider | Set by FSC per activity |
| **Cayman** | CIMA — Virtual Asset (Service Providers) Act | Full VASP licensing (Phase 2 live since April 2025) | Set by CIMA; substance expected |

**Seychelles** is the most commonly used. Its **Securities Dealer License** authorises forex, CFD, derivatives and crypto-CFD dealing from a single licence with roughly **US$100,000** minimum capital, and the FSA confirmed in early 2025 that crypto-CFDs are permitted under it without a separate VASP licence ([Zitadelle](https://www.zitadelleag.com/services/investment-licensing/seychelles-fsa)). But the **VASP Act 2024** (in force since September 2024) added real substance requirements — live system walk-throughs, genuine local operations, and a Code of Corporate Governance effective January 2026 ([Zitadelle](https://www.zitadelleag.com/news/seychelles-crypto-license-offshore-vasp-setup)).

**Cayman** is the most institutionally credible offshore option — and it shows in the numbers: only a handful of full VASP licences had been granted by early 2026, reflecting a deliberately high bar under CIMA.

<Callout title="Registration ≠ permission to do anything">
An offshore VASP *registration* is not a global licence. It authorises specific activities under that jurisdiction's rules — and it does **not** let you serve customers in markets that require their own authorisation. You still can't lawfully offer leveraged crypto derivatives to retail consumers in the US, the EU, or the UK from an offshore shell.
</Callout>

## The trade-offs you're really signing up for

Offshore can be a legitimate route — but be clear-eyed about the costs:

- **Banking and payment access.** The single hardest problem. Tier-1 banks and payment processors are wary of offshore crypto entities; expect limited, expensive fiat rails.
- **Market access limits.** You cannot legally onboard retail from the major regulated markets. Your addressable market is narrower than the "global" pitch suggests — see how the [US treats perpetual futures](/blog/us-perpetual-futures-cftc-regulation).
- **Reputational and counterparty risk.** Institutional partners, market makers, and auditors increasingly screen for jurisdiction. An offshore flag can cost you deals.
- **Tightening and grey-list risk.** FATF pressure has pushed every serious offshore jurisdiction to add substance and AML requirements; the compliance gap versus onshore is closing.

## When offshore makes sense (and when it doesn't)

Offshore can suit a venue serving eligible non-retail or non-restricted markets, or as a staging step while a fuller onshore licence is pursued. It's a poor fit if your growth plan depends on retail users in regulated markets, on institutional liquidity partners, or on smooth fiat banking.

## The takeaway

Offshore licensing is legitimate but no longer a shortcut around compliance — Seychelles, BVI and Cayman all now expect substance, AML programs and governance. Choose based on where your *customers* legally are, not just where incorporation is cheapest, and weigh banking access heavily. For regulated-market alternatives, compare Dubai's [VARA](/blog/uae-vara-crypto-derivatives-license), [Abu Dhabi's ADGM](/blog/adgm-fsra-crypto-derivatives-license) and the rest in our [global licensing guide](/blog/crypto-derivatives-licensing-global-guide), and build a real [KYC/AML program](/blog/kyc-aml-for-crypto-exchanges) wherever you land.
