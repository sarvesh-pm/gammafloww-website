Australia has quietly become one of the more demanding places to run a crypto derivatives business, because ASIC has long treated crypto **derivatives as financial products** — which means an **Australian Financial Services Licence (AFSL)**, the same regime that governs traditional finance. In 2026 that net widened to crypto platforms generally, and the timeline is tight.

## The new Digital Assets Framework

Australia's Parliament passed the **Corporations Amendment (Digital Assets Framework) Act 2026** (Royal Assent 8 April 2026), bringing **Digital Asset Platforms** and tokenised-custody providers under AFSL licensing ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/cryptocurrency/australia-moves-to-regulate-crypto-platforms-as-parliament-passes-bill-for-afsl/)). ASIC's guidance on how digital assets map to financial products sits in its updated **INFO 225** ([ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/find-a-document/consultations/cp-381-updates-to-info-225-digital-assets-financial-products-and-services/)).

A **small-platform exemption** applies (broadly, holding under A$5,000 per customer and under A$10m annual transactions), but any venue offering leverage or derivatives is well outside that.

<Callout title="The AFSL cliff (2026 deadlines)">
Transitional relief is narrow: a business generally must have been operating in Australia on or before **31 December 2025** and lodge a complete AFSL application by **30 June 2026**. Miss it and, from **1 July 2026**, you risk operating in breach of the Corporations Act. The full framework commences **9 April 2027**. If Australia is on your map, the licensing clock is already running.
</Callout>

## Derivatives and retail leverage

Crypto derivatives were largely kept *outside* the new platform-licensing carve-outs — meaning they continue to be treated as financial products requiring the appropriate AFSL authorisations, not swept into a lighter regime. ASIC has also been aggressive on **retail leverage**: it restricts leverage on crypto-asset CFDs offered to retail clients, and enforcement is real — the local operator of Kraken (Bit Trade) was fined **A$8 million** in December 2024 over a leveraged "margin extension" product ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/cryptocurrency/australia-moves-to-regulate-crypto-platforms-as-parliament-passes-bill-for-afsl/)).

## What it means for operators

- **Assume you need an AFSL.** A crypto derivatives venue serving Australians is a licensed financial-services business, full stop.
- **Mind the retail line.** Leverage to retail is constrained; a professional/wholesale focus is simpler.
- **Move on timing.** The transitional deadlines have largely passed for new entrants — plan a full AFSL path with local counsel.

## The takeaway

Australia regulates crypto derivatives as financial products under ASIC's AFSL regime, now reinforced by the 2026 Digital Assets Framework and a hard licensing timeline. Retail leverage is restricted and enforcement is active. Treat Australia as a full-licence market, not a light-touch one — and compare it with the [US](/blog/us-perpetual-futures-cftc-regulation) and [Singapore](/blog/singapore-mas-crypto-derivatives-license) routes in our [global licensing guide](/blog/crypto-derivatives-licensing-global-guide).
