**A crypto exchange development company builds, licenses, or customizes the platform your trading venue runs on — and the one you pick determines whether you launch on solid infrastructure or spend the next year firefighting.** Choosing well is the single highest-leverage decision in the whole project. Here's how to evaluate a development partner, and the red flags that should end a conversation.

## What to evaluate

**1. Track record with your product.** Have they shipped venues like yours — spot, or [derivatives](/blog/crypto-derivatives-exchange-development) specifically? Derivatives are far harder, and generic exchange builders often lack a production-grade [risk and liquidation engine](/blog/risk-and-liquidation-engines). Ask for live references, not logos.

**2. Engine quality.** The [matching engine](/blog/inside-a-crypto-matching-engine) is the product. Ask about latency, throughput under load, determinism, and how it behaved during real market crashes. Vague answers here are disqualifying.

**3. Liquidity.** A great engine with an empty book still fails. Do they provide or connect [liquidity](/blog/crypto-exchange-liquidity-solutions), and with whom?

**4. Security & compliance posture.** How do they handle [custody](/blog/crypto-exchange-custody-models), key management, and [security](/blog/crypto-exchange-security)? What's covered by them vs. you? Confirm this in writing.

**5. Support & roadmap.** 24/7 operational support, SLAs, and a maintained product — not a one-time delivery you're then left to run alone.

**6. Commercial transparency.** A clear [pricing model](/blog/white-label-exchange-pricing-models) (setup + monthly / revenue-share) with no hidden operational costs.

## Red flags

- **A fixed "$X and you're live" quote** with no discovery — real projects are scoped.
- **No named liquidity or references** — "we'll sort it out later."
- **Hand-waving on the risk/matching engine** — the parts that actually move money.
- **"We'll build it custom, cheap and fast"** — pick two; safety-critical exchange software isn't cheap *and* fast *and* good.

<Callout title="Development company vs. white-label provider">
These overlap. A "development company" may custom-build for you; a [white-label provider](/blog/how-to-choose-white-label-exchange-provider) licenses a ready platform you configure. For most operators the white-label route is faster and lower-risk — so evaluate whether you actually need custom development at all, or a proven platform to operate. Either way, run formal [vendor due diligence](/blog/white-label-exchange-vendor-due-diligence).
</Callout>

## The takeaway

Choosing a crypto exchange development company comes down to proof, not pitch: a real track record with your product, a matching and risk engine that survives load, liquidity you can name, and clear commercials. The best decision most operators make is realizing they need a *proven platform to run*, not a bespoke build — and picking the partner who can prove it works. First, understand the [full development landscape](/blog/cryptocurrency-exchange-development); then choose accordingly.
