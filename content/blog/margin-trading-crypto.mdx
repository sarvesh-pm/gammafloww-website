**Margin trading means putting up a fraction of a position's value — the margin — and effectively borrowing the rest, so you can trade a larger size than your own capital would allow.** It magnifies both gains and losses, and if the market moves against you far enough, your position is liquidated. Here's how it actually works in crypto, and where the risk lives.

## The core idea

When you buy on margin, your own funds act as collateral for a larger exposure ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/b/buying-on-margin.asp)). If you post $1,000 as margin and trade a $5,000 position, you're at 5x leverage — a 1% move in the market becomes a 5% move on your capital, in either direction. Margin is the mechanism; [leverage](/blog/crypto-leverage-explained) is the result.

## Margin vs. leverage vs. liquidation

These three go together:

- **Margin** — the collateral you post to open and hold a position.
- **Leverage** — the multiple of exposure that margin buys you (5x, 10x, up to 125x on some venues).
- **Liquidation** — what happens when losses eat into your margin past the maintenance threshold: the venue force-closes the position to stop the loss going negative ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/m/margin.asp)). Knowing your [liquidation price](/blog/how-liquidation-price-is-calculated) before you enter is the single most important habit in margin trading.

## Isolated vs. cross margin

How your collateral is allocated changes your risk profile completely:

- **Isolated margin** — collateral is fixed to one position. If it's liquidated, you lose only what you allocated to it. Losses are contained.
- **Cross margin** — your whole balance backs your positions, so a winning trade can support a losing one — but a bad move can put your entire balance at risk.

Which to use depends on the trade; the trade-offs are covered in [Isolated vs. Cross Margin](/blog/isolated-vs-cross-margin).

<Callout title="Margin cuts both ways">
Leverage doesn't just multiply profit — it multiplies loss and pulls your liquidation price closer to the current price. At 10x, a ~10% adverse move can wipe out the position. The higher the leverage, the smaller the move it takes to be liquidated. Size accordingly.
</Callout>

## Why traders use it

Used with discipline, margin lets a trader put capital to work more efficiently, hedge an existing position, or take a directional view without tying up the full notional. In crypto, most margin trading happens through [perpetual futures](/blog/what-are-perpetual-futures), where margin, leverage, and funding all interact. The tool is neutral; the risk comes from oversizing.

## The takeaway

Margin trading is borrowing to trade bigger — powerful and unforgiving in equal measure. The essentials: understand that leverage multiplies losses as well as gains, always know your liquidation price, and choose isolated or cross margin deliberately rather than by default. Master those and margin becomes a tool rather than a trap.
