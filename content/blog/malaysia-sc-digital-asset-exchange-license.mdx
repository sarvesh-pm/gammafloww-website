Malaysia has one of Southeast Asia's clearer crypto-exchange regimes — and one worth understanding alongside [Singapore](/blog/singapore-mas-crypto-derivatives-license) and [Hong Kong](/blog/hong-kong-sfc-vatp-crypto-derivatives) when you're mapping the region. Crypto exchanges are regulated by the **Securities Commission Malaysia (SC)**, which treats digital assets as securities and licenses trading venues as **Digital Asset Exchanges (DAX)** operating as **Recognized Market Operators (RMOs)**.

## How the framework works

The SC prescribes digital currencies and digital tokens as **securities**, which brings them under its capital-markets remit. A venue that wants to let people trade them must register as an **RMO to operate a DAX** under the SC's **Guidelines on Recognized Markets** ([SC Malaysia](https://www.sc.com.my/regulation/guidelines/recognizedmarkets)). Only registered operators may run a DAX, and the SC publishes the list — **six** DAX RMOs were registered as of late 2025 ([SC Malaysia](https://www.sc.com.my/regulation/guidelines/recognizedmarkets/list-of-registered-digital-asset-exchanges)).

## Capital and the May 2026 tightening

A DAX operator must maintain **minimum paid-up capital of RM5,000,000** (or foreign-currency equivalent) throughout its operations, and the SC can require more depending on the scale and complexity of the business.

In May 2026 the SC issued **revised Recognized Markets guidelines** (effective 20 May 2026) that raised the bar on financials, shareholding and management proficiency, strengthened safeguarding of client assets, and improved operational resilience ([SC Malaysia](https://www.sc.com.my/resources/media/media-release/sc-issues-revised-guidelines-on-recognized-markets-for-digital-asset-exchange)). DAX operators also became members of the Financial Markets Ombudsman Service in 2026, giving investors a formal dispute-resolution route.

<Callout title="Spot venue — derivatives are a separate question">
The DAX/RMO regime is built for **spot** trading of prescribed digital assets. Leveraged crypto **derivatives** for retail are not part of this framework — derivatives sit under separate capital-markets licensing and are tightly restricted. If your product is perpetual futures, treat Malaysia as a spot-first market and get specific advice on whether and how any derivatives offering is permissible.
</Callout>

## What operators should take away

- **Clear but not light.** The RMO route is well-defined, but RM5m capital plus the 2026 governance and client-asset rules make it a serious application.
- **Digital assets = securities.** That framing shapes everything — listing, disclosure, custody and conduct obligations follow securities logic.
- **Know the product boundary.** Spot DAX is licensable; retail crypto derivatives are not straightforward. Scope your offering to the regime before committing.

## The takeaway

Malaysia offers a defined path to a licensed **spot** crypto exchange — register as an SC Recognized Market Operator running a DAX, hold at least RM5m in capital, and meet the tougher post-May-2026 standards. Just don't assume it extends to retail derivatives; that's a separate, much narrower question.
