If you're weighing where to license a crypto derivatives venue in the UAE, the choice usually comes down to two regulators: Dubai's [VARA](/blog/uae-vara-crypto-derivatives-license) and Abu Dhabi's **ADGM**, supervised by the **Financial Services Regulatory Authority (FSRA)**. ADGM is the older, more institutionally-minded of the two — it launched the region's first comprehensive virtual-asset regime in 2018 and was, in its own words, "the first regulator globally to regulate platforms that enable the trading of Virtual Assets as Multilateral Trading Facilities" ([ADGM](https://www.adgm.com/business-areas/digital-assets)).

## What ADGM is (and why the distinction matters)

ADGM is a **financial free zone** operating under **English common law**, with the FSRA as its financial-services regulator — a structure closer to the DFSA in Dubai's DIFC than to VARA. That common-law footing, plus an early and bespoke rulebook, is why ADGM has become the venue of choice for institutional and global digital-asset firms.

Practically, that means a crypto exchange is authorised the same way any market infrastructure is: through a **Financial Services Permission (FSP)** for a specific regulated activity, not a standalone "crypto licence."

## The permission a derivatives exchange needs

Operating a trading venue maps to **Operating a Multilateral Trading Facility (MTF)** — running a platform where multiple buyers and sellers trade under non-discretionary rules. FSRA expects MTF operators to maintain exchange-grade controls: market surveillance, fair and orderly trading, settlement, transaction recording, rulebooks and transparency ([ADGM](https://www.adgm.com/business-areas/digital-assets)).

The key point for a *derivatives* venue: FSRA doesn't treat crypto derivatives as an afterthought. **"Derivatives over Digital Assets, and Collective Investment Funds investing in Digital Assets, are regulated as Derivatives and Units in a Fund by the FSRA"** ([ADGM](https://www.adgm.com/business-areas/digital-assets)). So a perpetual-futures venue needs the appropriate permission(s) for dealing in and operating a facility for those derivatives — expect this to sit on top of, not instead of, the market-infrastructure requirements.

<Callout title="Accepted Virtual Assets">
FSRA runs an **Accepted Virtual Asset** framework: a venue can only offer tokens the regulator has assessed against criteria like market maturity, security and liquidity. Your product roadmap (which pairs you list) is therefore partly a regulatory question, not just a commercial one.
</Callout>

## Capital and prudential expectations

FSRA sets prudential requirements by activity. As a general anchor, applicants are expected to hold capital equivalent to **at least six months of projected operating expenditure**, and MTF operators sit at the top of the base-capital scale — an indicative **US$250,000** base requirement is the common reference point for a virtual-asset MTF, layered with the expenditure-based minimum and any client-asset add-ons.

Treat these as indicative: FSRA amends its digital-asset rules regularly ([King &amp; Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/news-and-insights/adgm-fsra-implements-amendments-to-its-digital-asset-regulatory-framework)), so confirm the current figures for your exact permissions directly with the regulator and qualified counsel.

## ADGM vs. VARA at a glance

| | **ADGM (FSRA)** | **VARA** |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Abu Dhabi (free zone) | Dubai (excl. DIFC) |
| Legal basis | English common law | UAE federal law |
| Established | 2018 (regional first-mover) | 2022 (first dedicated VA authority) |
| Typical fit | Institutional, global firms | Retail-facing, regional operators |
| Exchange permission | Operating an MTF | Exchange Services VASP licence |

Both are FATF-aligned on AML/CFT, and **neither licence passports into the other's jurisdiction** — an ADGM permission doesn't authorise you in Dubai, and vice versa. Pick based on your target customers, product, and how much institutional credibility matters to your go-to-market.

## The takeaway

ADGM offers a mature, common-law, institution-friendly route to a licensed crypto derivatives venue, anchored on the **Operating an MTF** permission with derivatives explicitly in scope. It tends to suit operators prioritising global credibility over speed-to-retail — the mirror image of VARA's profile. Map your activity to the right FSP early, and confirm current capital requirements with FSRA.
