**Hedging crypto means taking an offsetting position that gains when your main holding loses, so a price drop hurts less — you're trading away some upside in exchange for protection on the downside.** It's risk management, not speculation: the goal isn't a new profit, it's insurance on the position you already have. Here are the main ways traders do it.

## What hedging actually is

A hedge is an investment made to reduce the risk of adverse price moves in another asset — you accept a smaller potential gain in return for a smaller potential loss ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/h/hedge.asp)). If you hold spot Bitcoin and want to protect it through a volatile stretch without selling, a hedge lets the holding ride while capping the pain of a fall.

## The main tools

- **Short a perpetual future.** Open a short [perpetual](/blog/what-are-perpetual-futures) against your spot holding. If the price falls, the short gains roughly what your spot loses. This is the most direct hedge — but it also caps your upside, since a rally costs you on the short ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/s/short.asp)).
- **Buy a protective put.** A [put option](/blog/what-is-a-put-option) sets a price floor: you pay a premium, and if the market crashes, the put pays off. Unlike a short, it keeps your upside — you only lose the premium if the drop never comes.
- **Go delta-neutral.** Size a short (or option position) so your *net* directional exposure is zero — a [delta-neutral](/blog/delta-neutral-strategy) hedge that removes price risk entirely while you hold the asset for other reasons (staking, funding, long-term conviction).

## Full hedge vs. partial hedge

You don't have to hedge everything. A **full hedge** neutralizes the whole position (and its whole upside); a **partial hedge** protects only a portion, leaving some exposure to a rally. Most traders hedge partially — enough to sleep, not so much that they've effectively sold.

<Callout title="Hedging has a cost">
Protection is never free. A short caps your upside; a put costs a premium; both take capital and attention. Hedging is worth it when the peace of mind or the avoided drawdown outweighs that cost — not as a permanent state. The point is to survive volatility, not to eliminate it.
</Callout>

## The takeaway

Hedging crypto is buying insurance on a position you want to keep: short a perp for a direct, cost-free-but-upside-capping hedge; buy a put to set a floor while keeping upside; or go delta-neutral to remove price risk altogether. Each has a cost, and the right one depends on what you're protecting against and for how long. Used deliberately, hedging is how serious holders ride out volatility instead of panic-selling into it.
